## Executive finding

The new calculations identify two exact, useful bookkeeping identities:

\[
  \frac12G_N'=T_N-\nu D_N,
  \quad T_N=\langle\omega_N\cdot S_N\omega_N\rangle,
  \quad G_N=\langle|\omega_N|^2\rangle,
  \quad D_N=\langle|\nabla\omega_N|^2\rangle;
\]

and, for a self-adjoint Fourier square partition with
\(\sum_jP_j^2=I\) on nonzero modes,

\[
  T_N=\sum_j\bigl(Q_{j,N}+R_{j,N}\bigr),\qquad
  Q_{j,N}=\langle P_j\omega_N,S_NP_j\omega_N\rangle,
  \quad R_{j,N}=\langle P_j\omega_N,[P_j,S_N]\omega_N\rangle.
\]

These identities establish an exact decomposition; they provide no
cutoff-uniform upper bound. In particular, \(Q_j\) includes the **full
strain** \(S_N\), generated by modes throughout the field. Calling it
stretching *within an isolated shell* would obscure that dependence.
The signed commutator \(R_j\) restores terms missed when the \(Q_j\)
are added separately. Neither \(Q_j\) nor \(R_j\) is required to be
positive. The data do not show that the two must always be estimated
together; a valid separately derived bound would also work.

## Claim and evidence register

| Finding | What is established | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| Spectral-power parity | For \(u\mapsto-u\), Fourier powers and power-derived entropies are fixed, while the cubic transfer \(T\) changes sign. Hence they cannot determine the *exact signed transfer* for all fields. | An even, conservative majorant can still bound \(T_+\); parity does not disqualify it. |
| Incompressible phase freedom | Conjugate scalar rotations of each \(\pm k\) coefficient preserve reality and \(k\cdot a_k=0\) instantaneously. A tested triad attains different signed transfers as its relative phase changes. | These phases are not independent during Navier–Stokes evolution. A single vector Fourier coefficient has no canonical scalar phase without a chosen frame or projection. |
| Gradient majorants | The standard derivative-dependent chain produces a cutoff-independent instantaneous upper envelope. | The available energy identity does not supply the additional time integral required to close this large-data \(H^1\) argument. Numerical slack in an envelope is not evidence of a uniform better inequality. |
| Finite-cutoff trajectories | The same aligned two-scale field was evolved to \(t=.02\) at \(N=4,5\), \(\nu=.1\). Its positive transfer rises over this interval, and endpoint values depend on cutoff. | Neither trajectory establishes a general phase-randomization principle, a blowup mechanism, or asymptotic convergence/divergence as \(N\to\infty\). |
| Smooth filtering | A square Littlewood–Paley partition gives exact shell energy and \(H^1\) accounting. The earlier gradient commutator sums to zero globally; the complementary gradient stretching terms sum to \(T\). | The shell identities alone do not control \(T_+\) over time. |
| Strain eigenframe | \(T=\sum_{r=1}^3\langle\lambda_r|\omega\cdot e_r|^2\rangle\) for the sorted eigenvalues of \(S\). On the sampled field, most vorticity weight lies along the middle eigendirection while positive net transfer grows. | This example does not disprove geometric depletion for all flows, establish an eigenvalue sign change, or verify a geometric regularity criterion. |
| Cross-shell strain remainder | \(T=\sum_j(Q_j+R_j)\) follows from self-adjointness and \(\sum P_j^2=I\); its Fourier kernel includes factors \(1-\sum_j\psi_j(k)\psi_j(q)\). | No useful cutoff-uniform time integral bound for the signed total has been obtained. |

The logical scope of the parity result matters: failure to predict a
signed quantity does not prevent a phase-blind functional from safely
overestimating its positive part. Conversely, individual numerical
failures of a chosen bound do not exclude the entire class of static
geometric conditions or all analytic approaches to large-data regularity.

unboundedness, or an uncontrolled asymptotic error. They do establish
that omitting \(R\) changes the accounting substantially *for this run*.

## Conditional proof gate, and what would make it nontrivial

A sufficient large-data route would prove, for the Galerkin solutions
of each fixed smooth initial datum and every finite \(T_*\),

\[
 T_N(t)\leq\frac{\nu}{2}D_N(t)+a_N(t)G_N(t),\qquad
 a_N\geq0,\quad
 \sup_N\int_0^{T_*}a_N(t)\,dt\leq C(u_0,\nu,T_*)<\infty.
\]

The estimate must be independently deduced from quantities that are
already controlled, with constants uniform over \(N\). It yields
\(G_N'\leq2a_NG_N\) and thus a cutoff-uniform bound on \(G_N\) for
each finite time horizon; standard continuation and compactness
arguments could then be applied. Defining \(a_N\) from the unknown
transfer merely restates the desired bound. Deriving such an estimate
from only the energy identity for arbitrary data would resolve a
substantial part of the open problem; no such derivation appears here.

The next rigorous task is to propose a specified functional involving
\(S\), \(\omega\), and the full cross-frequency interactions, then prove
or refute its estimate under amplitude and frequency scaling and along
Galerkin evolution. More runs can falsify candidate inequalities; they
cannot replace the proof of a cutoff-independent time integral.

## Prior work, terminology, and source map

Miller's middle-strain-eigenvalue paper gives conditional blowup
criteria involving specific scale-critical norms of \(\lambda_2^+\)
in its \(\mathbb R^3\) formulation; our eigenframe snapshots do not
verify those conditions for the periodic problem. Constantin and
Fefferman's result requires assumptions on vorticity direction in
high-vorticity regions; our alignment weights address a different
question. Tao's localization paper establishes implications among
global regularity formulations using localized energy and enstrophy
estimates. It does not prove that dynamic phase cancellation provides
an automatic universal bound.

Here `X=Σ_{k≠0} exp(2σ|k|)|k|^{2s}|a_k|²`,
`Y=Σ_{k≠0} exp(2σ|k|)|k|^{2s+2}|a_k|²`, and
`Z=Σ_{k≠0} exp(2σ|k|)|k|^{2s+1}|a_k|²`. The multiplier
itself is `m(k)=exp(σ|k|)|k|^s`; its **square** appears in X and Y.
Do not put `2s` inside the multiplier used in the commutator.

For the full symbol, PR #25 derives exactly

    N_{σ,s} = −Re <Au,[A,u·∇]u>,
    ([A,u·∇]u)^∧_k
      = i Σ_{p+q=k}(q·a_p)[m(k)−m(q)]a_q.

The difference `m(k)−m(q)` has polynomial and exponential contributions.
For square-partition symbols `m_j=ψ_j m`, the exact shell summands use
`m_j(k)−m_j(q)` and add to N. This is **not** an already established
decomposition into independent “intra-band stretching,” “Sobolev
remainder,” and “Gevrey remainder” mechanisms. One can split the symbol
difference algebraically in several ways, but each term needs its own
definition and validation before numerical ratios or physical labels
are assigned. The unweighted strain commutator in the earlier record
is a different identity and cannot replace this Gevrey H^s expression.

The phase tracker reports the instantaneous ratio
`Q=|Σ w_k Im(z_{kpq})|/Σ w_k|z_{kpq}|`, with the same ordered triads
in both sums. In the combined perturbed persistence-weight example at
t=0.005, Q is approximately 0.0704 (N=4) and 0.4083 (N=7), a difference
of **33.8 percentage points**. This is a normalized global signed
transfer diagnostic at s=2. It is not local vortex-stretching efficiency,
an explanation of the N-dependence, or a trend as N approaches infinity.
The ratio of a proposed remainder to N would become ill-conditioned
when the signed N crosses zero; absolute values and zero-denominator
handling would need to be specified before such a dashboard was useful.

### Precise sufficient target and the initial layer

At fixed radius σ=0, a sufficient yet **unproved** arbitrary-data
target for every finite T is

    ∫₀ᵀ N_{0,s}(t)dt ≤ θν∫₀ᵀY_{0,s}(t)dt + F_s(T,ν,u₀),
    0≤θ<1,

with F_s finite, specified solely from initial data and parameters,
and independent of N and unknown solution norms. Combining it with
the exact identity would leave `(1−θ)ν∫Y` and bound X on that finite
interval. Proving this for all smooth data would be a substantive
global-regularity result; the finite Galerkin identities do not prove it.
The statement “∫N is finite” without a uniform, non-circular F_s is
automatic at each fixed cutoff and has no such consequence.

For a growing radius, an additional bound on `∫σ'Z` is needed. Under
`σ(t)=α sqrt(νt)`, σ' grows like `t^(−1/2)` near zero. This derivative
is locally integrable; its divergence does **not** imply that a solution
or its analyticity radius breaks down at initialization. A direct
Young-inequality estimate instead creates a coefficient `(σ')²/ν`
behaving like `1/t`, which is a problem for that **particular proof
route** unless the initial layer is treated differently. As a simple
check, the linear heat equation has Fourier multiplier
`exp(−νt|k|²)` and obeys

    X_{α sqrt(νt),s}(t) ≤ exp(α²/2) X_{0,s}(0)

for every t>0: maximize `2α sqrt(νt)|k|−2νt|k|²` over |k| to get
`α²/2`. Thus the singular σ' by itself cannot be evidence against
parabolic analyticity. For the nonlinear equation, the reservation
inequality and radius-term control must be proved together, uniformly
in N, before claiming a continuum lower analyticity radius.

**External review target:** verify the exact symbols, projection and
shell partition; distinguish finite arithmetic closure from an a
priori estimate; identify every use of an unknown trajectory norm in
F_s; and check the near-zero radius argument separately. No claim of
global regularity, forced-case breakdown, or novelty of classical
Gevrey methods is made by this addendum.
