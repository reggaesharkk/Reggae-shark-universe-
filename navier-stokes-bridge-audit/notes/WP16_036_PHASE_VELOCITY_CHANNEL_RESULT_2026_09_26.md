**Prince Upadhyay, Independent Research — 26 September 2026**

For the dominant explicit finite triad

[
p=(3,2,2),qquad q=(3,-2,1),qquad k=(6,0,3),
]

the nonlinear phase velocity

[
dotalpha=Im(dot z/z)

[
dot a_p,qquad dot a_q,qquad dot a_k.

## Result

The channel driving local de-alignment is not fixed across the tested states.

At N9 target-only:

[
dotalpha_p=+83.15,qquad
dotalpha_q=-210.35,qquad
dotalpha_k=-154.00,
]

so the (q)-channel is largest in magnitude.

At N10 target-only:

[
dotalpha_p=+37.23,qquad
dotalpha_q=-201.55,qquad
dotalpha_k=+60.84,
]

again with the (q)-channel dominant.

At N11 target-only, after the optimizer has moved the dominant triad close to its favorable phase,

[
dotalpha_p=+34.95,qquad
dotalpha_q=-172.57,qquad
dotalpha_k=+571.21.
]

The (k)-channel now dominates and drives the net phase velocity strongly positive:

[
dotalpha=+433.59.
]

The full-final N11 state shows the same output-channel dominance.

Thus the finite local de-alignment mechanism changes character: the advected-mode channel is important at earlier cutoffs, while the output-mode channel becomes dominant in the strongly aligned N11 state.
